Three ways Inquora could look at itself

Same conversation, same three attached documents, same tool calls. Only the topology, density and typographic voice change. Pick the one that feels closest, and say what is wrong with it.

Each direction is rendered in its own dominant theme. Every one ships both themes if chosen; showing six panels here would compare themes instead of directions. The surface under test is the chat screen rather than the landing page, because PRODUCT.md puts the personality in the seams rather than behind the text, and a register that survives a transcript will survive a hero.

Direction A Archival cataloguing · limestone · light

The Ledger

Evidence is not a panel you open. It is a second column that fills up as you work, where every cited passage becomes a numbered specimen that stays on screen after the answer has scrolled past.

Topology

Two permanent columns. Conversation left, accumulating evidence right. Neither collapses.

Second register

Specimen numbering, hairline rules, catalogue labels. The visual language of a collection.

Why it holds

It makes the product thesis structural. Verification is not a click away, it is permanently in view.

Risk

Two columns halve the reading width. On a laptop the answer gets cramped, and the rail can feel like homework.

Direction B Marginalia · dark · critical edition

The Margin

There are no messages. The conversation is a set text with a wide margin, and everything procedural, who spoke, what ran, how long it took, lives out there where a scholar would put it.

Topology

One reading column with a fixed 156px gutter. No bubbles, no avatars, no alternating sides.

Second register

Marginalia against precise setting: rules, brackets, pulled quotes, a rose underline where a source resolves.

Why it holds

Reading is the primary act and this is the only direction that treats the transcript as something to read rather than operate.

Risk

Closest to the second-order reflex I warned about in PRODUCT.md. Dark ground and rose pull it away from editorial-serif-on-paper, but only just.

Coverage held. It was measured on the wrong thing.

The review states plainly that coverage is calculated on unweighted opportunity value1, which does not adjust for a falling average deal size. Opportunity count rose 18% across the quarter, so the ratio stayed comfortably above target while the value behind it thinned.

Coverage remained above the 3.0x target throughout the quarter. Coverage is calculated on unweighted opportunity value, which does not adjust for the observed decline in average deal size. revenue-review-q3.pdf · page 4

The spreadsheet confirms the shape of it: enterprise average deal size fell to $39,600, 21.4% under plan2, while mid-market finished 4% ahead and absorbed part of the gap.

Neither document says why deal size fell. That is not answerable from what is attached.

Direction C Plotter plate · two pens · light

The Workbench

The tools are the interface. A search shows its query and its ranked hits, a table query shows its SQL and its result grid, and both stay in the transcript as instruments rather than collapsing into a spinner.

Topology

Document bench across the top, dense thread below, sources docked right. Horizontal, not columnar.

Second register

Technical plates: two plotter pens, measurement bars, dotted rules, tabular figures throughout.

Why it holds

It is the only direction that shows the thing no competitor has. Tool calls become the reason to trust the answer.

Risk

Density is a taste. It reads as a professional instrument to one person and as a Bloomberg terminal to the next, and it is the least calm of the three for a one-hour reading session.
