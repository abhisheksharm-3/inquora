The Apparatus

A straight mashup would have failed. B's gutter sits left, A's evidence rail sits right, and three columns crush the reading measure at laptop width, which is exactly the risk named against A.

They are the same thing. B's gutter and A's rail both carry material that supports the text without being the text. Folded into one right-hand column, the left becomes pure reading, and the layout goes back to two columns.

A critical edition sets the text on the page and the apparatus criticus beside it: sources, variants, notes, everything scholarly. Tool calls and citations are apparatus. The name is borrowed from the thing this already is, not invented for it.

Kept from A

Numbered specimens that persist after the answer scrolls, an accumulating record, catalogue labelling, the oxide mark.

Kept from B

No bubbles. The transcript is a set text in Newsreader, procedural detail exiled from the reading column, timings on the record.

New in the hybrid

Operations and specimens interleave chronologically in one column, so the apparatus reads as what happened as well as what backs it.

Both themes

Shown light and dark below. The oxide mark shifts lightness between them rather than changing hue, so it stays the same colour idea on both grounds.

Why is Q3 revenue 12% under forecast when pipeline coverage was above target all quarter?

Coverage held. It was measured on the wrong thing.

The review states that coverage is calculated on unweighted opportunity value1, which does not adjust for a falling average deal size. Opportunity count rose 18% across the quarter, so the ratio stayed comfortably above the 3.0x target while the value behind each opportunity thinned.

The spreadsheet confirms the shape of it. Enterprise average deal size landed at $39,600 against a plan of $50,400, 21.4% under2, and carried almost the whole miss. Mid-market finished 4.1% ahead and absorbed part of the gap without closing it.

Neither document says why deal size fell. That question is not answerable from what is attached.

What is still unresolved

  1. The apparatus at 1280px. 330px of apparatus plus a 58ch reading column needs about 1150px. Below that the apparatus has to collapse to a rail of specimen numbers that expand on click, and that collapsed state is a design problem, not a media query.
  2. Whether operations stay visible forever. Twenty turns in, the apparatus is mostly timings nobody reads. Options: fade operations after the turn completes and keep specimens, or let the column filter to specimens only.
  3. Where the document viewer goes. Clicking specimen 1 should reach page 4 of the PDF. That is a third surface, and it either replaces the apparatus, overlays the text, or opens beside both, which brings the three-column problem back.
  4. The second register is still thin. Serif text against mono apparatus is a real collision, but it is a typographic one. Whether that is enough personality, or whether something drawn belongs in the empty and first-run states, is the remaining question.
